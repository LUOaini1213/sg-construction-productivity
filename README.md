Personal desk study on public data; not a BCA study.

# sg-construction-productivity

A desk study of construction productivity in Singapore and of what Design for Manufacturing and Assembly
(DfMA) can and cannot be shown to do, using open government data and single figures published by the
Building and Construction Authority (BCA). It has three parts: a productivity trend from SingStat series, a
check of what the open data can say about DfMA, and a transparent illustrative model of one hypothetical
residential block built conventionally, with a precast frame, with precast plus prefabricated MEP, and with
PPVC (prefabricated prefinished volumetric construction), including a line-of-balance (takt) comparison of
floor cycles. A static Plotly dashboard shows the results.

This README is generated by `scripts/build_readme.py` from `results/*.json`; a test fails if any number in it
does not come from a results file.

## Results

### 1. Construction productivity trend (open data)

Series definitions (SingStat with MOM, SSIC 2020): value added (VA) per worker is construction gross value
added in chained (2015) dollars divided by employment (table M015761); VA per actual hour worked is the same
value added divided by actual hours worked (table M015851). Neither is a site measure: both cover the whole
construction industry, including civil engineering. There is no open series per man-day; the man-day figure
below is VA per hour times an assumed 8-hour day.

| Measure | Value | Results file |
|---|---|---|
| Construction VA per actual hour, 2025 | S$18.38 (S$15.56 in 2009) | `results/productivity_trend.json` |
| Same, as % of the whole-economy figure | 28.6% (37.7% in 2009) | `results/productivity_trend.json` |
| Log-linear trend growth of construction VA per hour, 2009-2025, excluding 2020 and 2021 | 0.93% a year, bootstrap 95% interval 0.54 to 1.31 | `results/productivity_trend.json` |
| Same, all years kept | 0.77% a year (0.19 to 1.32) | `results/productivity_trend.json` |
| Compound growth of construction VA per hour, 2009 to 2019 / 2019 to 2025 | 1.70% / -0.03% a year | `results/productivity_trend.json` |
| Whole economy VA per hour, compound growth 2009 to 2025 | 2.82% a year (construction: 1.05%) | `results/productivity_trend.json` |
| Construction VA per worker, 2020 vs 2019 | -39.2% | `results/productivity_trend.json` |
| VA per man-day equivalent, 2025 | S$147 (unit conversion, not a published series) | `results/productivity_trend.json` |
| Implied actual hours per construction worker, 2025 (VA per worker / VA per hour) | 2,211 hours a year | `results/productivity_trend.json` |

Reading: construction productivity per hour grew slowly before 2020, has been roughly flat since 2019, and
fell further behind the rest of the economy. The trend interval is wide because there are only
15 annual points, and it moves when the
pandemic years are kept or dropped.

### 2. What the open data can show about DfMA

| Measure | Value | Results file |
|---|---|---|
| BCA DfMA adoption, 2019 to 2024 | 31% to 68%, 37 points | `results/dfma_evidence.json` |
| Construction VA per hour over the same years | -2.6% | `results/dfma_evidence.json` |
| Correlation of adoption with VA per hour | r = -0.29 on n = 4 (5% critical value 0.95) | `results/dfma_evidence.json` |
| Contracts awarded, 2025 (current prices) | S$50,656 million, 51% above 2019 | `results/dfma_evidence.json` |
| Building work share of contracts awarded / progress payments, 2025 | 81.7% / 75.4% | `results/dfma_evidence.json` |
| Construction, marine and process work permit holders, Dec 2022 | 415,000 (2019 to 2020: -15.9%) | `results/dfma_evidence.json` |

The adoption figures are four single figures from BCA releases (2019, a September 2022 "today" figure, 2023,
2024); the bases differ (the 2019 figure does not say "by GFA"). With four points a correlation of any sign
is uninformative, and the observed one is not significant. The open data cannot separate a DfMA effect from
the pandemic, foreign-worker supply and the demand boom shown above. A further reason it cannot: factory work
for precast and PPVC is counted in manufacturing, or abroad, not in Singapore construction value added, so a
shift off-site changes who is counted in the denominator as well as how efficient the work is.

### 3. Illustrative model: one hypothetical block, four methods

All parameters are in `params/model.json`. Every one is a labelled assumption with a low and a high bound
(the high bound of `prefab_mep_time_cut` equals BCA's published "up to" figure for prefab MEP); none is calibrated to a project. BCA's
published figures are used only as checks on the output. Off-site factory labour is outside the model, so
"site productivity" is GFA per site man-day. Base case: 10,400 m2 GFA.

| Method | Site man-days | Duration, days | Site m2 per man-day | Manpower saving | Duration saving, days | Takt, days per floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional | 20,800 | 293 | 0.50 | - | - | 8.0 |
| Precast frame | 18,252 | 291 | 0.57 | 12.2% | 2 | 8.0 |
| Precast + prefab MEP | 17,004 | 288 | 0.61 | 18.2% | 4 | 8.0 |
| PPVC | 12,716 | 237 | 0.82 | 38.9% | 56 | 5.0 |

Uncertainty (`results/model_montecarlo.json`, 2000 draws, every assumption
triangular between its bounds, independent): PPVC site manpower saving
29.5% to 46.5%
(5th to 95th percentile, median 38.5%); duration saving
21 to 125 days;
site productivity ratio 1.42 to 1.87
times conventional. The bounds are the author's, so these bands show the spread implied by the assumptions,
not a confidence interval.

One-at-a-time sweep (`results/model_sensitivity.json`): the PPVC manpower saving is most sensitive to
`ppvc_structure_labour_cut`, `share_substructure`
and `ppvc_arch_labour_cut`; the duration saving to
`conv_arch_days`, `ppvc_finish_days`
and `floors`.

Checks against BCA's published PPVC figures (`results/model_base.json`):

| Model output (PPVC, base) | Model | BCA figure | Within? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site manpower saving | 38.9% | 20% (case study) to "up to 40%" | yes |
| Time per floor cut | 39.2% | "up to 30%" | no |
| Duration saving | 1.84 months | 1 to 2 months on average | yes |

The time-per-floor cut is above BCA's upper figure, so either the model's on-site PPVC trades are too fast or
its conventional finishing trade is too slow; the data here cannot tell which. It is reported, not tuned away.

### 4. Lean angle: line of balance

Each trade has one crew that works up the building floor by floor; a trade cannot start a floor until the
previous trade has finished it plus a buffer (`src/scp/model.py`, `line_of_balance`). The schedules are in
`results/model_lob.json` and drawn in the dashboard.

- Conventional: the floor cycle is set by the slowest trade, architectural works at
  8 days a floor, not by the structural cycle. Cutting the
  structural cycle with a precast frame saves 12.2% of
  site man-days but only 2.1 days, because the
  structure was not the bottleneck.
- PPVC moves most trades into the factory; the site line is module install, connections and finishing, with
  a takt of 5.0 days a floor and a superstructure of
  87 days against
  143. The bottleneck is on-site
  finishing, not the crane or the factory: the factory makes one
  floor of modules every 4.0 days, faster than the
  finishing takt, and at the slow end of the factory rate the stock built during the lead time covers the gap,
  so in the one-at-a-time sweep `factory_lead_days` and `factory_modules_per_day` do not change
  the duration at all. In this model, PPVC programme time is governed by what is still done on site.

## What the data does not support

- Any causal statement that DfMA raised (or failed to raise) construction productivity in Singapore. The
  industry series and four adoption figures cannot separate DfMA from COVID-19, labour supply and demand.
- Any site-level or project-level productivity figure. There is no open per-project or per-man-day series.
- Anything about PPVC, precast or prefab MEP performance on a real project: the model is illustrative and its
  parameters are assumptions.
- Construction-only foreign workforce numbers: the open MOM dataset combines construction, marine and process
  work permits and ends in 2022.
- Real (inflation-adjusted) demand: contracts and progress payments are in current dollars.

## Dashboard

`dashboard/index.html` is a static Plotly page generated by `scripts/build_dashboard.py` from `results/`. It
loads plotly.js from a CDN; the figure data is embedded in the page. **It was not built in Power BI.** A Power
BI project (PBIP/TMDL) was the preferred format but Power BI Desktop was not driven for this repo, so the
dashboard is Plotly. `tests/test_dashboard.py` re-parses the HTML and checks that every card value and plotted
value equals the number recomputed in Python from `data/` and `params/`.

## Reproduce

```
python -m venv .venv
.venv/Scripts/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt   # .venv/bin/python on Linux/macOS
.venv/Scripts/python scripts/compute_results.py
.venv/Scripts/python scripts/build_dashboard.py
.venv/Scripts/python scripts/build_readme.py
.venv/Scripts/python -m pytest
```

`scripts/fetch_data.py` re-downloads the data and needs internet; nothing else does. The test suite blocks
network sockets.

## Tests and mutation testing

`tests/` covers parsing, the trend and correlation maths, the line-of-balance schedule against hand-worked
cases, parameter labelling, results reproducibility, dashboard reconciliation, README provenance and repo
hygiene. `scripts/mutate.py` plants 31 bugs one at a time and runs the suite
against each; three tests that read the mutation report or this README are left out of those runs. First run: 30 of 31 killed.
Final run: 31 of 31 killed (`results/mutation.json`).
